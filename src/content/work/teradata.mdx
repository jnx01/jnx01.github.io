## Context

Teradata builds data and analytics platforms used by large enterprises. I work
on the GenAI and Agentic AI team, where the central challenge is turning
natural-language questions into correct, executable SQL against real
enterprise schemas, a problem that has to work outside the notebook, under
production constraints, for customers who cannot afford wrong answers.

Over three years I've worked across several distinct production AI systems
rather than a single product: an enterprise Text-to-SQL agent, the agentic
skills platform, the schema-mapping engine, and the VectorStore retrieval
pipeline. This case study covers them in turn.

Because this is production enterprise software, everything here stays at a
safe level of abstraction: no client names, no internal architecture, nothing
that isn't already public.

## The Text-to-SQL agent

My deepest ownership was the **API layer of the enterprise Text-to-SQL
agent**, the seam between the backend and the rest of the product. I designed
and built roughly forty REST endpoints across two FastAPI services, covering
prompt handling, metadata ingestion, script execution, job orchestration, and
initialization. I took this layer from before the product launched through
the onboarding of its first enterprise customers.

## The agentic skills platform

On a separate track, I **authored agentic skills for Teradata's AI
platform**, most notably a train-eval skill for in-database ML training,
evaluation, and interpretation, invoked through MCP tool calls. These skills
are validated in CI with LLM-graded correctness and tool-use metrics, so a
regression in how the agent reasons shows up as a failing test, not a
surprised customer.

## Evaluation as a through-line

The part of the work I'm most proud of is that evaluation isn't an
afterthought; it's the method, and it runs across every project I touched.
A few examples:

- **Schema mapping.** Working on the AI-driven schema-mapping engine, I
  improved accuracy by systematically comparing LLM councils, retrieval
  strategies, embedding models, and prompt designs for automated
  client-to-IDM mapping.
- **Retrieval robustness.** With the VectorStore team, I helped harden the
  RAG pipeline: in-database embedding generation, HNSW vector search, and
  extending support for custom tokenizers and small language models.
- **CI as a safety net.** I built the CI pipeline (60 automated tests, 75%
  coverage) and resolved more than 35 critical bugs, then led
  deployments across AWS and on-premises environments.

Earlier internal work included benchmarking modulation-classification models
with in-database ML at scale (feature augmentation across tables on the
order of a trillion rows), which is where I learned what “large” really means
for enterprise data.

## Outcomes

These systems reached enterprise customers ahead of general availability,
including analytics and migration work for one of the world's largest banks.
I partnered with those customers and with engineering teams to onboard them,
delivered consulting solutions for earlier clients, authored customer-facing
documentation and notebooks, and mentored three junior engineers. I was
promoted twice in under two years: from Data Science Trainee Consultant to
Data Science & ETL Consultant I to Data Science Engineer II.

## What I took from it

Shipping AI to enterprises taught me that the model is the easy part. The
hard part is everything around it: the API contract, the harness,
the deployment story, and the discipline to treat a probabilistic system like
production software. That lesson is exactly what my independent research on
efficiency and reliability digs into.
